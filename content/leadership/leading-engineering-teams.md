# Leading and Managing Engineering Teams

## Introduction
Engineering leadership is creating the conditions for a team to deliver valuable, maintainable software and grow its judgment. The output is not the leader's personal code; it is team outcomes, clarity, capability, and sustainable execution.

```text
Clear goal + context
Team ownership and decision space
Unblocked execution
Feedback/growth
Reliable delivery and healthier team
```

## Purpose
Teams need clear priorities, realistic constraints, fast blocker removal, useful feedback, psychological safety, and accountability. Leadership balances delivery with capability and team health; ignoring either eventually harms both.

## Real-world simple example
A team repeatedly misses sprint commitments. Before demanding more effort, inspect the system:

```text
Missed work
  ├── unclear acceptance criteria?
  ├── external dependency?
  ├── work too large?
  ├── hidden operational load?
  ├── one person overloaded?
  └── priorities changing mid-sprint?
```

Fix the cause: clarify scope, escalate the dependency, slice work, rebalance ownership, or reduce competing priorities.

## Professional company-level example
A senior lead creates a team operating model:

- One clear quarterly outcome and measurable success criteria.
- Explicit decision owners for architecture/product/operations.
- Regular 1:1s focused on growth and blockers, not status reporting.
- Review standards that separate correctness from preference.
- On-call/incident ownership shared and sustainable.
- Technical debt visible and prioritized against product risk.
- Delegated opportunities that grow engineers' scope.

The lead still contributes technically, but does not become the permanent bottleneck for every difficult decision.

## Delegation model

```text
Low risk + reversible → delegate fully
Medium risk            → delegate with checkpoints
High risk/irreversible → lead decision with transparent reasoning
```

Delegation is not abandonment. State the outcome, constraints, decision authority, check-in points, and what support is available.

## Common failure scenarios
- Leader solves every hard problem personally and creates a bottleneck.
- 1:1s become status meetings.
- Feedback arrives only during performance reviews.
- Team metrics reward activity instead of outcomes.
- Psychological safety is discussed but dissent is punished.
- “Autonomy” is given without context or decision boundaries.

## Comparison
| Mode | Useful when |
|---|---|
| Directive | Crisis, safety, inexperienced team/clear urgent action |
| Coaching | Building judgment and ownership |
| Delegating | Capable owner, reversible/known work |
| Facilitative | Team has expertise but needs alignment |

## Interview Questions
- **[L1]** What is the difference between managing and leading?
- **[L1]** How do you remove blockers for a team?
- **[L2]** How would you handle repeated missed commitments?
- **[L2]** How do you delegate without abandoning someone?
- **[L3]** When do you make a technical decision yourself versus delegating it?
- **[L3]** How do you balance delivery pressure with team health and long-term capability?

## Interview Answers
1. Managing focuses on planning, accountability, and process; leading focuses on direction, trust, judgment, and growth. Effective senior roles do both.
2. Find whether the blocker is a dependency, decision, unclear requirement, missing skill/tool, or priority conflict, then remove it or escalate with a specific recommendation.
3. Investigate the system before blaming effort, fix the root cause, reduce scope or dependency risk, and create a realistic plan with transparent communication.
4. State the outcome, constraints, authority, support/checkpoints, and expected escalation path; let the owner make the decision where risk is appropriate.
5. Delegate reversible/low-risk choices; stay involved in high-blast-radius/irreversible decisions, but explain reasoning so the team develops judgment.
6. Make commitments and capacity visible, protect sustainable focus, invest in mentoring/automation, and communicate trade-offs when business pressure exceeds safe capacity.

## Expert perspective
The best evidence of leadership is not that the team depends on the leader for every hard problem. It is that the team makes good decisions, communicates risk early, and keeps improving when the leader is not in the room.
