# Engineering Leadership Learning Path

## Introduction
This path develops the communication, coaching, decision, delivery, and people skills required to lead engineering work without becoming a bottleneck.

## Learning sequence

```text
Team leadership
   ↓
Mentoring/coaching
   ↓
Code review/feedback
   ↓
Stakeholder communication
   ↓
Conflict resolution
   ↓
Technical decisions
   ↓
Agile delivery
   ↓
Performance/career growth
```

## Daily leadership outcome
You should be able to clarify outcomes, remove blockers, delegate safely, give useful feedback, communicate risk, resolve conflict, make trade-offs, improve team process, and support career growth fairly.

## Interview Questions
- **[L1]** What is the purpose of engineering leadership?
- **[L1]** Why is specific feedback better than vague feedback?
- **[L2]** How do you delegate without abandoning someone?
- **[L2]** How do you communicate delivery risk?
- **[L3]** How do you balance delivery pressure and team health?
- **[L3]** How do you make decisions while preserving dissent and accountability?

## Interview Answers
1. Create clarity, trust, capability, sustainable delivery, and good decision conditions for the team.
2. It identifies observable behavior, impact, expected change, and a practical next step.
3. Define outcome/constraints/authority/checkpoints/support, then reduce support as ownership grows.
4. Communicate early with impact, evidence/uncertainty, options, recommendation, and decision/resource ask.
5. Make capacity/risk visible, protect focus/sustainability, and offer scope/time alternatives instead of silently accepting unsafe commitments.
6. Hear dissent, define criteria/owner, decide transparently, record reasoning, and follow up with evidence rather than requiring endless consensus.

## Expert perspective
Leadership is measured by the team's judgment and resilience, not by how many decisions or difficult tasks require the leader personally.
