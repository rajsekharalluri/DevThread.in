# Performance Reviews and Career Growth

## Introduction
A performance review should summarize an ongoing feedback relationship, not reveal months of surprise criticism. Career growth should connect a person's goals to observable behaviors, scope, impact, and opportunities.

```text
Regular evidence/feedback
         ↓
Review summary
         ↓
Specific growth expectation
         ↓
Opportunity + support
         ↓
Evidence/revisit
```

## Purpose
Fair reviews improve performance, retention, and trust. Vague feedback such as “show more ownership” is not useful unless it becomes specific behavior and a path to demonstrate it.

## Real-world simple example
Instead of:

```text
“Communication needs improvement.”
```

Say:

```text
“During the API redesign, the mobile team learned about the breaking change
only after implementation started. For the next design, identify affected teams
and circulate the contract before implementation begins.”
```

The second version describes observable behavior, impact, and a practical improvement.

## Professional company-level approach
Evaluate against role/level expectations using evidence across the entire period:

- Technical/business impact.
- Ownership and follow-through.
- Decision quality and communication.
- Collaboration/mentoring.
- Reliability/operational responsibility.
- Scope and complexity handled.
- Learning/adaptation.

Avoid recency bias, visibility bias, personality similarity, and comparing people directly instead of against a consistent standard.

## Career growth
A useful growth conversation answers:

```text
What does the person want?
What behavior/scope demonstrates the next level?
What opportunity will provide evidence?
What support/checkpoint exists?
When will both revisit progress?
```

A promotion is not “try harder”; it is evidence that the person is already operating at the next level consistently, with scope appropriate to the role.

## Interview Questions
- **[L1]** Why should feedback not be saved for the formal review?
- **[L1]** What makes performance feedback actionable?
- **[L2]** How do you reduce recency bias?
- **[L2]** How do you make a career-growth conversation concrete?
- **[L3]** How do you evaluate fairly across people with different roles and visibility?
- **[L3]** How do you handle a serious performance concern before the formal cycle?

## Interview Answers
1. Prompt feedback preserves context and gives the person time to improve; surprise feedback at review time is unfair and hard to act on.
2. Specific observation, impact, expected behavior, and a practical next opportunity/checkpoint.
3. Keep lightweight evidence throughout the entire review period instead of relying on recent memory and recent events.
4. Connect the person’s goal to level expectations, observable scope/behavior, a real opportunity, support, and a review date.
5. Evaluate against consistent expectations for each role/level, adjust for opportunity/visibility, use multiple evidence sources, and avoid personality-based comparisons.
6. Address it promptly and directly with specific examples, support/expectations, documentation, and the appropriate formal process; do not wait for a scheduled review to deliver important feedback.

## Expert perspective
A review process cannot compensate for absent feedback culture. Good leadership makes performance expectations visible, discusses concerns promptly, and makes growth evidence-based rather than mysterious or personality-driven.
